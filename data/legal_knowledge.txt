SECTION 10 OF THE INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872 – What agreements are contracts

All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not expressly declared void under law.

SECTION 37 OF THE INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872 – Obligation of parties to contracts

The parties to a contract must either perform, or offer to perform, their respective promises unless such performance is excused under the provisions of law.

Promises may also bind representatives of the parties in certain situations unless the contract indicates otherwise.

SECTION 39 OF THE INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872 – Effect of refusal of party to perform promise wholly

If a party to a contract refuses to perform or becomes incapable of performing their promise entirely, the other party may terminate the contract unless they have accepted the continuation of the contract through words or conduct.

A party affected by such refusal may also be entitled to compensation for damages arising from the failure to perform contractual obligations.

SECTION 63 OF THE INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872 – Promise may dispense with or remit performance of promise

A promisee may wholly or partly waive, reduce, or extend the performance obligations of another party under a contract. A promisee may also accept partial satisfaction instead of complete performance.

This may include temporarily allowing delayed payments or extending payment deadlines without immediately terminating the agreement.

Such acceptance may discharge the original obligation either fully or partially depending on the agreement between the parties.

SECTION 73 OF THE INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872 – Compensation for loss or damage caused by breach of contract

When a contract is broken, the party suffering from the breach is entitled to receive compensation for losses or damages that naturally arise from such breach or were reasonably foreseeable when the contract was made.

Compensation is not granted for remote or indirect losses arising from the breach.

In assessing compensation, the means available to reduce or remedy the inconvenience caused by non-performance may also be considered.

SECTION 74 OF THE INDIAN CONTRACT ACT, 1872 – Compensation for breach of contract where penalty is stipulated

When a contract specifies a penalty or a fixed amount payable in case of breach, the affected party is entitled to receive reasonable compensation not exceeding the amount or penalty stated in the contract, even if actual loss is not fully proven.

Penalty clauses and increased charges arising from default may be treated as stipulations by way of penalty under law.

Courts may determine reasonable compensation depending on the circumstances of the breach.
